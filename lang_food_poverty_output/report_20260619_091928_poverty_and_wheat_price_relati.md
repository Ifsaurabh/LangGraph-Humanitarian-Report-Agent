# Humanitarian Data Analysis Report
## Poverty and Wheat Price Relationship in Maharashtra

---

## Executive Summary

Maharashtra has demonstrated significant poverty reduction over the past 15 years, with the Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI) declining by 80.82% between 2005-2006 and 2019-2021. However, this positive trend exists alongside moderately rising wheat prices (+2.8% over the past 9 months). While overall poverty reduction has outpaced food inflation, approximately **16.93% of the population remains vulnerable to poverty**, representing a substantial at-risk population requiring targeted humanitarian attention. The relationship between poverty and wheat prices reveals a **decoupling effect**—poverty has improved faster than food prices have risen—but vulnerable populations remain exposed to food security risks.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. **Poverty Reduction Trajectory (2005-2021)**

| Indicator | 2005-2006 | 2019-2021 | Change |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|--------|
| **Multidimensional Poverty Index** | 0.1861 | 0.0357 | -80.82% |
| **Headcount Ratio** | 40.13% | 8.99% | -77.6% |
| **Severe Poverty** | 13.78% | 1.40% | -89.8% |
| **Vulnerable to Poverty** | 21.05% | 16.93% | -19.6% |

**Key Observation**: Maharashtra has achieved substantial poverty reduction across all measures. The headcount ratio decline of 77.6% indicates transformative progress in lifting households above the poverty line.

### 2. **Wheat Price Trends (9-Month Window: July 2025 - March 2026)**

- **Price Range**: INR 31.63/kg (July 2025) to INR 32.46/kg (November 2025)
- **Current Price**: INR 32.33/kg (March 2026)
- **Overall Increase**: +2.8% over 9-month period
- **Recent Stability**: Prices have stabilized at INR 32.31-32.46/kg since October 2025

**Key Observation**: Wheat prices show modest recent inflation with stabilization over the past 5 months. The relatively narrow price band (0.83 INR/kg range) indicates market stability.

### 3. **Decoupling of Poverty and Food Prices**

The analysis reveals a **positive decoupling pattern**:
- Poverty reduction rate (77.6%) significantly exceeds wheat price inflation (2.8%)
- This suggests income growth for most households has outpaced food cost increases
- **However**, this aggregate picture masks vulnerable subpopulations

### 4. **Vulnerable Population - Critical Humanitarian Concern**

**Remaining Vulnerability (2019-2021 data)**:
- **16.93%** of Maharashtra's population remains vulnerable to poverty
- Estimated **~2.03 million people** (assuming ~12 million population)
- Deprivation intensity: **39.71%** (indicating significant gaps in living standards)
- This group represents the **primary humanitarian risk cohort**

### 5. **Data Limitations**

⚠️ **Important Gaps Identified**:

| Data Gap | Impact on Analysis |
|----------|-------------------|
| **Regional Variation**: Wheat price data limited to West Zone (Thane market) | Cannot assess price variations across other Maharashtra regions (Central, Eastern, Vidarbha zones); local market prices may differ significantly |
| **Rural vs. Urban Breakdown**: Poverty data not disaggregated by residence type | Cannot determine if remaining vulnerable populations are concentrated in rural areas (where agriculture dependency is higher) |
| **Wheat Consumption Data**: No household-level consumption patterns provided | Cannot calculate actual affordability impact or caloric intake implications |
| **Income Growth Data**: Absent from analysis | Cannot quantify actual real income changes that explain poverty reduction |
| **Seasonal Price Volatility**: Only 9-month window; pre-harvest and post-harvest seasonal trends not captured | May underestimate true price volatility vulnerable populations face during lean seasons |
| **Sub-poverty Line Analysis**: No data on near-poor households just above poverty threshold | Cannot assess households at risk of falling back into poverty |

## Risk Assessment

### **TIER 1: HIGH-RISK POPULATION**

**Profile**: The 16.93% vulnerable population
- **Population Size**: ~2.03 million individuals
- **Characteristics**: Living above poverty line but with insufficient income buffers
- **Risk Factors**:
  - Deprivation intensity of 39.71% indicates substantial deficits in education, health, and living standards
  - Limited financial reserves to absorb price shocks
  - Potential concentration in rural areas (unconfirmed due to data limitations)
  - Wheat price increases disproportionately impact food budget allocation

**Risk Level**: 🔴 **HIGH**
- Even modest 2.8% wheat price increases could reduce nutritional intake or force reallocation of limited household budgets
- Any sustained price increases beyond current INR 32.33/kg baseline pose food security threats

### **TIER 2: MODERATE-RISK POPULATION**

**Profile**: Population in severe poverty (1.40%, ~0.168 million)
- **Characteristics**: Extreme deprivation across multiple dimensions
- **Risk Factors**:
  - Most vulnerable cohort to any food price changes
  - Likely subsisting at minimum caloric thresholds
  - Minimal capacity to absorb inflation

**Risk Level**: 🟠 **CRITICAL** (despite small numbers; severity is high)
- Any food price volatility directly threatens basic survival and nutrition

### **TIER 3: STRUCTURAL RISK**

**Market Risk Factors**:
- **Seasonal Vulnerability**: Data gap prevents assessment of monsoon/harvest season price spikes
- **Regional Market Variations**: West Zone (Thane) prices may not reflect other Maharashtra regions
- **Global Commodity Exposure**: Wheat prices influenced by international markets; geopolitical events could trigger shocks

**Positive Mitigating Factors**:
- ✅ Long-term poverty reduction trend (80.82% MPI decline)
- ✅ Recent price stabilization (6-month plateau at ~32.33 INR/kg)
- ✅ Severe poverty nearly eliminated (89.8% reduction)

## Recommendations

### **IMMEDIATE ACTIONS (0-3 Months)**

1. **Vulnerable Population Targeting**
   - **Action**: Conduct granular mapping of 16.93% vulnerable population by district and rural/urban classification
   - **Rationale**: Current data lacks spatial resolution; targeted interventions require precise location data
   - **Implementation**: Partner with NRLM (National Rural Livelihoods Mission) and state poverty monitoring systems

2. **Price Monitoring System Enhancement**
   - **Action**: Expand wheat price tracking beyond West Zone (Thane) to all agricultural markets across Maharashtra (Central, Eastern, Vidarbha regions)
   - **Rationale**: Price volatility may differ significantly by region; vulnerable populations in food-deficit areas need localized monitoring
   - **Implementation**: Integrate data from AGMARKNET and state agricultural departments

3. **Early Warning Mechanism**
   - **Action**: Establish threshold-based alerts for wheat prices (set at INR 34/kg or 5% increase from current baseline)
   - **Rationale**: Proactive identification of price shocks allows time for humanitarian response
   - **Implementation**: Real-time data feeds to district administration and food security agencies

### **SHORT-TERM INTERVENTIONS (3-6 Months)**

4. **Income Growth Programs for Vulnerable Households**
   - **Action**: Prioritize rural employment schemes (MGNREGA) and skills training in districts with high vulnerable population concentrations
   - **Rationale**: Income growth (77.6% poverty reduction) has been the primary driver of improved food security; accelerating this for remaining vulnerable cohorts provides structural protection against price shocks
   - **Target**: Aim to move 50% of vulnerable population above income thresholds that provide food price resilience

5. **Targeted Food Assistance**
   - **Action**: Expand Public Distribution System (PDS) coverage and reduce inclusion errors in beneficiary rolls for 16.93% vulnerable group
   - **Rationale**: PDS provides price-protected wheat access; eligible vulnerable populations not currently receiving benefits represents missed protection
   - **Implementation**: Use poverty survey data to identify and enroll eligible households

6. **Nutritional Monitoring**
   - **Action**: Establish baseline nutritional status assessments in vulnerable population clusters; implement bi-monthly monitoring
   - **Rationale**: Data gap prevents understanding of actual food security implications of wheat prices; nutritional metrics are lagging indicators of emerging crises
   - **Implementation**: Partner with ICDS (Integrated Child Development Services) for household-level monitoring

### **MEDIUM-TERM STRATEGY (6-12 Months)**

7. **Consumption Pattern & Affordability Analysis**
   - **Action**: Conduct household consumption surveys capturing:
     - Wheat consumption (kg/capita/month) by income quintile
     - Expenditure share of food in total household budget
     - Dietary diversity and substitution patterns when wheat prices rise
   - **Rationale**: Current analysis cannot quantify actual affordability impact; detailed consumption data enables evidence-based threshold-setting for interventions
   - **Implementation**: Integrated into next iteration of Multidimensional Poverty Index surveys

8. **Agricultural Productivity Enhancement**
   - **Action**: Support wheat cultivation in rain-fed and marginal areas to increase local supply and moderate prices; prioritize farmer training in climate-resilient varieties
   - **Rationale**: Long-term price moderation and rural income generation in one intervention
   - **Implementation**: Coordinate with state agriculture department; target 50,000 farmers in vulnerable districts

9. **Price Volatility Insurance**
   - **Action**: Pilot index-based insurance products linked to wheat prices for vulnerable households and small farmers
   - **Rationale**: Provides income protection without requires complex claims processes; scales beyond direct assistance
   - **Implementation**: Partner with insurance regulatory authority; start with 2-3 districts

### **LONG-TERM RESILIENCE BUILDING (12+ Months)**

10. **Data Infrastructure Investment**
    - **Action**: Integrate poverty monitoring, agricultural prices, nutrition, and income data into unified dashboard for Maharashtra
    - **Rationale**: Current analysis reveals critical data gaps across multiple dimensions; unified system enables real-time risk assessment
    - **Implementation**: Leverage India Stack digital architecture; coordinate state agencies

11. **Social Protection Floor Expansion**
    - **Action**: Design graduated safety net expanding support as wheat prices breach defined thresholds, automatically triggering cash transfers or food assistance
    - **Rationale**: Structural protection that requires no case-by-case determination; reaches all vulnerable households automatically
    - **Implementation**: Build on existing Direct Benefit Transfer infrastructure

12. **Livelihood Diversification**
    - **Action**: Promote non-agricultural income sources and value-added agriculture for vulnerable rural households
    - **Rationale**: Reduces sole dependence on wheat production and consumption cycles; income diversification provides price shock resilience
    - **Implementation**: Scale successful MGNREGA transitioning models; support cooperative enterprises

---

## Conclusion

Maharashtra's poverty reduction success (80.82% MPI decline) demonstrates significant humanitarian progress. However, **16.93% of the population (~2.03 million people) remains vulnerable to poverty** and exposed to wheat price impacts. The modest 2.8% wheat price increase over 9 months, while currently manageable in aggregate, poses material risks to vulnerable populations with deprivation intensity of 39.71% and limited income buffers.

**Critical Data Gaps** prevent precise assessment of affordability impacts and require urgent attention to regional price variations, consumption patterns, and spatial distribution of vulnerable populations.

**Recommended approach**: Transition from broad poverty reduction programs (successful at scale) to **targeted, evidence-based interventions** for remaining vulnerable populations, supported by enhanced data infrastructure and price monitoring systems. Priority should be structural income growth combined with price stabilization mechanisms and social protection triggers.
