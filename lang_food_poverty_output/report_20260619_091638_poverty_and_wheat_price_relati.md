# Humanitarian Data Analysis Report
## Poverty and Wheat Price Relationship in Bihar

---

## Executive Summary

This analysis examines the relationship between poverty levels and wheat prices in Bihar, India. While poverty has shown substantial improvement over the past 15 years (65.57% decline in Multidimensional Poverty Index), a significant vulnerable population remains. Wheat price data from 2023 reveals geographic disparities that create localized food security risks. However, **a critical limitation prevents definitive causal analysis: longitudinal wheat price data aligned with poverty measurement periods is unavailable**.

---

## Key Findings

### Poverty Trajectory (Positive Trend)
- **Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI) declined 65.57%** from 0.4484 (2005-2006) to 0.1544 (2019-2021)
- **Severe poverty headcount fell from 50.75% to 13.19%** over the same period
- This represents substantial progress in poverty reduction

### Persistent Vulnerability (Critical Gap)
- **19.98% of Bihar's population classified as "vulnerable to poverty"** (2019-2021)
- Estimated at **~2.4 million people** with minimal income buffers
- This group faces acute risk from price shocks

### Wheat Price Geographic Disparity (2023 Data)
- **Price range: 21.52 to 28.00 INR/KG** across districts
- **Average: 24.62 INR/KG**
- **Geographic variation: 30% spread** between lowest (Gaya) and highest (Bhagalpur) prices
- Highest-price districts (Bhagalpur, Banka) located in northern Bihar

### Food Affordability Risk Estimate
- Annual wheat consumption (typical household): ~180 kg
- At average price: **4,431.60 INR annually** for wheat alone
- Vulnerable population (19.98%) likely spends 25-40% of income on food, making wheat price sensitive

### **Data Limitation - Critical Gap**
**Wheat price historical data is unavailable.** Analysis contains only 2023 snapshot (July-August) data:
- No price trends for 2005-2006 or 2015-2016 periods
- Cannot establish temporal correlation with poverty reduction phases
- Cannot determine if poverty decline coincided with price stability or other factors
- Unable to assess whether vulnerable population faces worsening affordability over time

---

## Risk Assessment

| Risk Factor | Severity | Evidence |
|-------------|----------|----------|
| **Food Price Volatility** | HIGH | 30% district-level price disparity; vulnerable population has minimal shock absorption capacity |
| **Geographic Access Inequality** | HIGH | Bhagalpur (28.00 INR/KG) vs. Gaya (21.52 INR/KG); suggests supply chain inefficiencies |
| **Vulnerable Population Size** | HIGH | 19.98% (~2.4M people) classified as one shock away from poverty |
| **Income-Price Mismatch** | MODERATE | Assumed food spending patterns; actual household income data unavailable |
| **Data Continuity** | CRITICAL | No historical wheat price data; cannot establish causal poverty-price relationship |

### Causal Relationship: **UNESTABLISHED**
Poverty reduction from 2005-2016 cannot be confirmed as related to wheat prices due to missing historical price data. Multiple factors (agricultural productivity, policy interventions, economic growth) may have driven poverty reduction independent of wheat prices.

## Recommendations

### 1. **Immediate Data Collection (Priority: URGENT)**
   - Compile monthly/seasonal wheat price data for Bihar from 2005-2023
   - Cross-reference with poverty surveys (NFHS, World Bank MPI data)
   - Establish baseline for price-poverty correlation analysis

### 2. **Targeted Intervention for Vulnerable Population**
   - Focus food security programs on 19.98% vulnerable population in high-price districts
   - Monitor Bhagalpur, Banka districts for price shocks; establish price alert mechanisms
   - Strengthen public distribution system (PDS) in high-variance regions

### 3. **Geographic Equity Analysis**
   - Investigate causes of 30% price disparity (transportation, market structure, storage)
   - Improve supply chain efficiency in high-price districts
   - Consider market intervention to reduce geographic price inequity

### 4. **Household-Level Risk Mapping**
   - Correlate wheat price volatility with household vulnerability data by district
   - Identify intersections of high poverty vulnerability + high wheat prices
   - Design spatially targeted early warning systems for food security crises

### 5. **Future Research**
   - Establish ongoing wheat price monitoring linked to poverty surveys
   - Model price elasticity of food security outcomes for vulnerable populations
   - Assess impact of price shocks on vulnerable-to-poverty transition rates

## Conclusion

Bihar has achieved significant poverty reduction (65.57% MPI decline), yet **19.98% of the population remains vulnerable to poverty**—a group of approximately 2.4 million people. Current wheat prices (2023) show 30% geographic disparity, creating localized food security risks for vulnerable populations, particularly in Bhagalpur and Banka districts.

**However, the causal relationship between poverty and wheat prices remains unestablished due to absence of historical price data.** To enable evidence-based humanitarian interventions, longitudinal wheat price data (2005-2023) aligned with poverty measurement periods must be obtained and analyzed.

**Recommendation: Prioritize data collection on historical wheat prices before designing poverty-wheat price mitigation strategies.**
